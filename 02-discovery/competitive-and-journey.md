# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The Disillusioned Curated Seeker - A passionate viewer who values thoughtful recommendations and actively looks for new content, not just more content.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Discover hidden gems and personalized recommendations that reflect their tastes, mood, and context.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** "The 'Because you watched' row is a joke." Recommendations repeatedly surface near-duplicate titles and franchise content, causing the user to lose trust in the platform's ability to help them discover something genuinely interesting. This drives them toward competitors offering curated selections and human-guided discovery.

Evidence: Marcus (film buff), Tom (lapsed subscriber who moved to a competitor offering hand-picked recommendations), Beth (mood-based browsing needs), and Sam (trusts friends more than the algorithm).
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Because the recommendation system is no longer trusted, the Disillusioned Curated Seeker effectively builds their own discovery process outside of StreamLine. Rather than using the platform as the primary source of discovery, they use external curation and social validation to decide what to watch before returning to the service, if they return at all.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Step 1: Ignore Platform Recommendations

The user skips or distrusts rows such as "Because You Watched" because recommendations feel repetitive and overly focused on genre or franchise similarity.

Evidence

Marcus: "The 'Because you watched' row is a joke..."
Sam: "I trust a recommendation from a friend more than the algorithm."
Step 2: Seek Recommendations Elsewhere

The user consults alternative sources to identify worthwhile content.

Typical external sources include:

Friends and family recommendations
Social media discussions
Film review sites
Critics' lists and newsletters
Curated "Top Movies This Week" articles
Competitor services providing editorial recommendations

Evidence

Sam trusts friends more than the algorithm.
Tom moved toward a competitor that sends "two hand-picked films a week."
Step 3: Build a Personal Shortlist

The user manually creates a mental or written shortlist of content that appears promising.

Instead of asking:

"What should I watch on StreamLine?"

they ask:

"What should I watch anywhere?"

Only after deciding do they check whether the title is available on the platform.

Step 4: Return to StreamLine for Execution

The platform becomes merely a playback destination rather than a discovery destination.

The user arrives with a specific title already chosen and uses:

Exact-title search
Watchlist
Direct navigation

rather than browsing recommendations.

Step 5: Gradually Shift Discovery Habits Away from the Platform

Over time, discovery becomes permanently detached from the product.

Users increasingly rely on:

Competitor recommendations
Curated newsletters
Human tastemakers
Social networks

The platform's role shrinks to content hosting.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Users must invest time across multiple systems just to answer a simple question:

"What should I watch tonight?"

Instead of receiving a recommendation within seconds, they must research, compare, validate, and shortlist content themselves.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** "I can no longer trust StreamLine to help me find something worth watching, so I have to do the work myself."
